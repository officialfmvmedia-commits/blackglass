How it works

  1. We watch public wallets.Addresses come from official sanctions lists, FBI statements, analyst post-mortems and open exchange-label datasets.
  2. We flag unusual moves.Big transfers, sudden balance drops, money going to mixers or hacker wallets, and funds split fast across many new wallets.
  3. You get a plain-English alert with sources.Each alert links to the transaction and to where the label came from, so you can check it yourself.

Methodology

Terms

Tracked wallet
An address named by a public source. Every label links to it.
Hot / cold wallet
Hot wallets pay out withdrawals; cold wallets hold reserves. Big moves between them are often routine.
Score (0–100)
How closely a movement matches known hack and laundering patterns. A pattern match, not proof.
Peel chain
Funds moved step by step, peeling off a small slice each time.
Mixer
A service that pools coins to hide where they came from (e.g. Tornado Cash).
Bridge
A service that moves funds to another blockchain.
Fan-out
Money split across many new wallets very quickly.

How alerts are made

The pipeline runs every 10 minutes on GitHub Actions. It reloads the OFAC sanctions list, FBI-published addresses, DefiLlama's hacks list, the Elliptic Binance-hack blocklist and open exchange-label datasets, then reads recent transactions from Blockscout (Ethereum, Optimism, Polygon) and Blockstream (Bitcoin). Busy exchange wallets are checked in rotation. Prices come from DefiLlama.
